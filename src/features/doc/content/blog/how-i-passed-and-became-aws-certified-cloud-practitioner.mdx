This year, I made a promise to myself: I would finally start my cloud journey and move closer to my dream of working in Cloud Security. That’s how I ended up sitting for the AWS Cloud Practitioner exam. Four weeks ago, I passed.

But it didn’t start with confidence. It started with curiosity, a little pressure, and a clear goal.

## Why I chose the AWS Cloud Practitioner exam

I’ve always wanted to become a cybersecurity expert. But I kept hearing the same thing from professionals already in the field: “You need to understand the cloud.”

So, I picked AWS, the biggest cloud provider, and decided to begin with the Cloud Practitioner certification. It’s the entry-level cert, and I saw it as the perfect first step toward cloud security.

## My previous experience with AWS

Before I started studying, I had only used AWS once. That was three years ago. I was working with a client who needed a WordPress site hosted in the cloud. I used Lightsail and Route 53 to set it up. But that was it. I had no deep experience with AWS.

So, when I started reviewing the AWS Cloud Practitioner exam guide, it all felt new again.

## How I prepared for the exam

I gave myself 20 days to prepare. I wasn’t working full-time, so I treated studying like a job. Every day, I spent 2 to 3 hours learning.

I started with Andrew Brown’s AWS CCP course on YouTube. I liked it because it was straight to the point and easy to follow.

After finishing the videos, I started doing small hands-on projects. I created S3 buckets, set permissions, encrypted files, and explored IAM policies. These mini-labs helped me connect the theory with real AWS tools.

In the last few days, I took practice exams, starting with Andrew’s free ExamPro tests, then moving to a paid Udemy test. They were tough at first, but I reviewed every question I got wrong and learned from my mistakes.

## What I struggled with the most

There were moments I felt lost, especially when trying to understand what all the different AWS services do. So many names, so many acronyms. EC2, EKS, ELB, SQS. It was a lot.

What helped was this: I stopped trying to memorize everything. Instead, I started grouping services based on their purpose, like Compute, Storage, Security, and so on. That brought clarity.

## My real exam day experience

I booked my exam at a PearsonVUE center. I walked in feeling a bit nervous but ready. The moment I saw the first few questions, I knew: this is familiar. The practice tests had really prepared me. The questions weren’t easy, but they were fair. I finished the exam, submitted it, and waited.

Then came the result, I passed.

## Why this certification mattered to me

For some people, this is just an entry-level cert. But for me, it was a milestone. One of my 2025 goals, checked off. And more than that, it showed me I could set a short deadline, work consistently, and get results.

Now, I’m moving forward, planning to take AWS Solutions Architect Associate and AWS Security Specialty certifications, while diving deeper into cloud security.

## My tips for anyone preparing

If you’re preparing for the AWS Cloud Practitioner exam:

- Stick to one course at first. I recommend Andrew Brown’s YouTube course.
- Do hands-on practice. Even simple labs will help a lot.
- Take practice tests, review your weak spots, and make sure you can explain each service in simple terms.
- And most importantly, don’t overthink it. If you stay consistent, you can pass in 3 to 4 weeks.

This is just the beginning of my cloud journey, and I’m proud of the progress so far. If you’re reading this and getting ready to take the exam, I hope my story gives you the boost to stay focused and believe in yourself.

You’ve got this!
